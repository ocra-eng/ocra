OCRA Éireann

A rebuilt identity for the national governing body of obstacle sports — recognised by World Obstacle (FISO) and the European Obstacle Sports Federation. The symbol stays what it has always been: the triskele — kept whole, cleared of clutter, and given room to run.

Why change

The case

The current badge has the right symbol and the wrong everything else — a clip-art athlete, gymnastic rings, a flag-painted circle, and outlines that collapse below 40 pixels. The rebrand keeps the triskele and removes everything standing on it.

Today

  • Five competing elements fighting inside one circle
  • Tricolour used literally — reads as a flag sticker, not a brand
  • Fine outlines and the hanging figure vanish at bib and favicon sizes
  • Locked into a badge — nothing to build posters, kit, or a system from

Proposed — the triskele

  • The badge's own triskele, lifted out whole — nothing new invented
  • No outlines, no gradients, nothing to fall apart in print
  • Holds from a stadium banner down to a favicon
  • National identity carried by the symbol and the bilingual name, not a painted flag

Alternative — the badge, decluttered

  • The current badge exactly as drawn — athlete, rings and text removed
  • Keeps the tricolour and the spiral work the community already knows
  • Zero relearning: reads as a cleanup, not a rebrand
  • The most detailed option — happiest at 48 px and larger

Older than the pyramids, still in training. The triple spiral was cut into the entrance stone at Newgrange around 3200 BC and has read as motion ever since — three limbs turning forward, never resting. These are the exact spirals from the current badge, so nothing is thrown away: the athlete, the rings, and the flag fill come off, and the spiral finally stands on its own. It is used exactly as drawn, in one colour, at any size.

Clearspace

Keep one spiral-eye of empty space around the mark on all sides.

One colour

Bog on light grounds, chalk on bog or field green. Never multicoloured, never outlined.

Don't decorate

No athletes, gymnastic rings, or flag fills inside the ring. The symbol carries the brand alone.

Don't redraw

Use the master artwork as supplied. No rotation, mirroring, or restyling of the spirals.

Colour

Terrain, not flag

The palette comes off the course: bog, field, chalk grip, race tape. The tricolour survives as proportion, not as a flag — green carries, white breathes, orange is the ten percent that marks the route.

On any layout, green family ≈ 60%, chalk ≈ 30%, tape ≈ 10%. That ratio is the national identity doing its work quietly — nobody needs the flag painted on. The triskele itself always sits in a single colour from this palette.
