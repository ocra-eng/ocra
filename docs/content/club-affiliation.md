Affiliation creates a formal relationship between an OCR club and OCRA, and makes
the club part of the national structure of the sport.

## What it gives a club

Recognition as an OCRA Affiliated Club, inclusion in the
[club directory](/clubs/directory), and promotion through OCRA channels.

Access to [coaching](/coaching) and
[Technical Official](/education/technical-officials) development as those
pathways open. Club development support. Information on events, competitions,
National Championships and international qualification. Opportunities to
collaborate on OCRA programmes, and connections to other clubs and facilities.

:::not-live
Some of the above depends on programmes that are still being built — the coaching
and Technical Official pathways in particular. Affiliating now connects you to
them as they open rather than granting access to something finished.
:::

## Your club stays your club

Affiliation does not mean OCRA runs or controls the day-to-day activity of an
affiliated club. Clubs remain responsible for their own management, activities,
finances and members.

What it provides is a recognised relationship and a route into national decisions
about the sport — not a reporting line.

## Who can apply

OCR clubs and established obstacle training communities operating in Ireland.
Requirements depend on the structure and activities of the club, but expect to
provide the club name and location, contact details, how the club is structured,
what training you run, who coaches, your membership arrangements, and relevant
club policies.

Where the club runs activities for children or young people,
[safeguarding](/governance/safeguarding) arrangements are part of the application
rather than something to sort out afterwards.

## What is expected in return

Affiliated clubs are expected to operate in a way that supports safe, fair and
inclusive participation — respecting applicable OCRA policies and maintaining
appropriate standards within club activities.

## Working together

A strong national sport needs strong local clubs. OCRA wants affiliated clubs to
stay independent while working together where national coordination genuinely
helps: athlete and coach development, youth development, competition, technical
standards, safeguarding and community programmes.

## Applying

[Contact us](/about/contact) to discuss the current process.

If your club is new and you are not sure whether you are ready, get in touch
anyway — it is easier to build towards affiliation with a conversation than to
guess at requirements. [Starting a club](/clubs/start-a-club) covers the earlier
stage.
