OCRA membership makes you part of the national organisation for obstacle sport in
Ireland, and it is what funds the structures the sport needs to develop.

## What membership actually gets you

We would rather be exact about this than list benefits that do not exist yet.

Today, membership gives you:

- **Access to the Team Ireland pathway.** Membership is required to qualify for and
  be selected for international championships. See
  [qualification](/compete/qualification).
- **Eligibility for the Irish National Championships**, where the competition
  requires it.
- **Access to OCRA courses** and development opportunities as the
  [coaching](/coaching) and [Technical Official](/education/technical-officials)
  pathways open.
- **A say in how the sport is run.** Active members may attend the General
  Assembly, which elects the Board and approves changes to the
  [governing documents](/governance/constitution-bylaws).

:::not-live
Two things are commonly assumed and are not true yet: there is **no national
ranking system**, and there is **no member discount scheme**. When either exists it
will be listed here, not implied.
:::

## Where membership is required

Depending on the competition or programme, membership may be required for
international qualification, Team Ireland participation, National Championships,
and certain OCRA courses.

Where it is required, the relevant information says so explicitly. If you are
unsure whether you need it for something specific,
[ask before you pay](/about/contact).

## You do not have to be a competitive athlete

Plenty of members take part recreationally — training, entering events, learning
obstacle skills, meeting other athletes, and being part of the community. OCRA
exists for the development of obstacle sport, not only for championship athletes.

## What your membership funds

Athlete development and national competition. Team Ireland. The coaching and
Technical Official pathways. Club and community development. Qualification
pathways. Safeguarding and sporting standards. The governance that holds all of it
together.

It is a volunteer-led, non-profit organisation, so membership income goes to the
objectives of the sport — the [governance](/governance/structure) page sets out how
that is decided and accounted for.

## Categories and fees

Membership types, fees, eligibility and membership periods are shown as part of
registration, before you pay. You should be able to see exactly what type you are
buying, what it costs, how long it lasts, and any competition requirements
attached to it.
