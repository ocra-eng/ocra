Obstacle racing has always been built around community. People arrive because they
want to race, get fitter or learn something new. Most of the ones who stay do so
because of the friendships, clubs, training groups and shared misery of a wet
Saturday morning.

## Who is in it

Recreational, competitive and elite athletes. Age Group, youth and adaptive
athletes. Clubs and training groups. Coaches, Technical Officials, race organisers
and volunteers. Gyms and training facilities. And the families who stand in fields
holding coats.

Every one of those is load-bearing. The sport does not run without the last three.

## Ways in

Join a [club or find somewhere to train](/clubs/directory). Enter your first
[event](/compete/events). [Volunteer](/get-involved/volunteer). Become a
[coach](/coaching) or a [Technical Official](/education/technical-officials).
[Start a club](/clubs/start-a-club). [Join OCRA](/membership).

## Everyone had a first race

Every experienced OCR athlete was once standing at a start line with no idea what
they were doing.

You do not need to be an elite athlete, complete every obstacle, or arrive with
years of training behind you. There should be room in this sport both for people
chasing a podium and for people who just enjoy the training and the company — and
where there is not, that is worth telling us about.

## Clubs are the foundation

Local clubs and training groups are what make a sport sustainable. They give
people a reason to train between race weekends, somewhere to learn from people
further along, and a way to be involved beyond entering events.

OCRA wants stronger links between clubs around the country, and to help new ones
start where OCR has not reached yet.

## Events bring it together

Independent race organisers introduce thousands of people to obstacle racing every
year across a range of formats — see the [events calendar](/compete/events) for
who is running what.

OCRA works with those organisers while developing national competition,
qualification and athlete pathways.

## Volunteers

Obstacle sport runs on volunteers. They support competitions, clubs, community
activity, technical operations, athlete services, event delivery and OCRA
programmes.

Volunteering is also how a lot of people find their way into coaching,
officiating and event organisation. [Volunteer with OCRA](/get-involved/volunteer)
covers what is involved.
