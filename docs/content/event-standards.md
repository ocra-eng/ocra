OCRA does not want every Irish OCR event to look the same. A recreational mud run
does not need to operate like a National Championship, and a local competitive
race does not need the infrastructure of a major international one.

So these standards scale with the nature, size and status of the event. What they
exist to guarantee is narrower than it looks: where an event carries a formal OCRA
status, athletes and organisers can rely on appropriate standards having been met.
Everything beyond that is your call as the organiser.

## Safety

Safety is the organiser's responsibility at every level of event, sanctioned or
not.

A sanctioned competitive event is expected to have a risk assessment, medical
coverage suited to the event, safety signage, an emergency action plan, adequate
staffing, and Technical Officials where the competition requires them.

### Risk assessment

Identify the foreseeable hazards in your venue, course, obstacles, participants
and operations — course conditions, weather and surfaces, fall risk and water,
athlete flow, start and finish areas, participant numbers, spectators, emergency
access, equipment and staffing.

Do this as part of planning the event, not as paperwork produced afterwards to
show you thought about it.

### Obstacle safety

Obstacles should suit the participants and the format. Consider structural
suitability and installation, landing areas and surface conditions, how athletes
move through the obstacle, maintenance and inspection, what weather does to it,
and the volume of people using it across a day.

Competition obstacles have a second requirement: they must let officials apply the
rules consistently. An obstacle that can be completed three defensible ways is a
protest waiting to happen.

### Medical provision and emergencies

Medical arrangements should match the size, location and risk profile of the
event, with emergency access and communication planned in.

Everyone relevant needs to know, before race day: who holds responsibility, how
emergency services are contacted, how medical staff are alerted, how a given part
of the course is reached, how competition gets stopped if it has to be, and how
athletes and spectators are managed while that happens.

## Competition

### Technical Officials

Sanctioned competitive events may require trained
[Technical Officials](/education/technical-officials). How many, and at what
level, depends on the discipline, the course and the level of competition.

Officials are what make obstacle completion requirements, penalties, athlete
procedures and result classifications mean the same thing in every lane.

### Rules

Tell athletes which rules apply before they compete, not at the start line.

The rules need to be clear on race format, obstacle completion and retries,
penalties, course boundaries, timing, categories, result classifications, and how
a protest works. See [Rules & Regulations](/compete/rules).

### Timing and results

Competitive events need a reliable way to record performances. A result should
identify the athlete, their category, finishing position, time or score, any
penalties, and the classification.

Where an event feeds an OCRA qualification pathway, results have to be good enough
to verify officially. Settle the format with OCRA before the event rather than
after it.

### Athlete communication

Get the important information out before race day: registration details,
categories, start times, rules, any mandatory briefing, equipment requirements,
qualification criteria, the protest procedure, and how results will be published.

## Applying for a status

If you are considering sanctioning, Qualification Event status or a National
Championship, [Event Recognition & Sanctioning](/race-organisers/sanctioning) sets
out the review process and what each requires.

Talk to us early. It is considerably easier to build a standard in from the start
than to retrofit one three weeks out.
