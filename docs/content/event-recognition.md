Independent race organisers can connect their events to the national development
of obstacle sport at whatever level suits them. This page is the full explanation
of what each status means and how to get one — other pages link here rather than
repeating it.

## The four statuses

::include{from="_partials/event-statuses.md"}

## Listed events

A listed event stays entirely independent. Listing helps athletes find it, builds
a more complete national calendar, and connects the organiser to the wider
community.

It carries no sporting status. It does not mean OCRA has reviewed the competition,
and it does not make results usable for international qualification.

## Sanctioning

Organisers who want a formal sporting relationship apply for sanctioning, and OCRA
reviews the event against the requirements appropriate to the status being sought.

What gets looked at depends on the race format, the level of competition, the
athlete categories, the qualification status sought, Technical Official
requirements, safety arrangements, and the applicable sporting rules. See
[Event Standards](/race-organisers/standards).

## Qualification Event status

Where an international championship requires Irish qualification, only events
approved by OCRA for that purpose count towards it.

Qualification status can be narrow — it may apply only to specific disciplines,
categories, Age Groups, race distances or dates. **Sanctioning an event does not
make every race within it a qualification race.**

:::before-you-rely-on-this
An approved Qualification Event must be identified as one **before** athletes
compete. Nobody should discover after the fact that the result they were chasing
did or did not count.
:::

## Irish National Championship

OCRA runs National Championships directly or with an established organiser. An
event wanting to host one applies and is reviewed, and the bar covers sporting
integrity, competition management, Technical Officiating, safety, timing and
results, athlete communication and rules compliance.

## The review

Where sanctioning, qualification or championship status is requested, OCRA reviews
the event first. That covers the format, course and obstacles, competition rules,
athlete categories, Technical Officials, timing and results, safety arrangements,
medical provision, emergency procedures, organiser experience and event capacity.

## Approval and advertising it

Do not advertise an OCRA status until it is confirmed in writing. Once it is, the
event can display the applicable badge.

## Applying

[Contact us](/about/contact) with the details of the proposed event. Engage early
where qualification or championship status is in play — these are not decisions
that can be made well in the last few weeks before a race.
