A club gives you regular training, people to train with, someone to ask about the
obstacle you cannot do yet, and company on the drive to races. A gym with obstacle
equipment gives you somewhere to practise the technical skills a race will
actually test.

Most people end up using both.

:::not-live
The directory is being built as clubs affiliate and facilities are listed, so it
will fill up over time rather than arriving complete. If you run either and want
to be in it, [tell us](/about/contact).
:::

## Clubs

Irish OCR clubs and training groups vary a lot. Some are competitive squads. Some
are training groups that meet twice a week and go to two races a year. Most are
somewhere in between, and it is worth contacting a couple before you decide.

Each listing shows where the club trains and when, whether it runs youth
sessions, whether coaching is available, and how to get in touch.

Affiliated clubs appear here automatically — see
[club affiliation](/clubs/affiliation).

## Training facilities

OCR training combines running, strength, grip, climbing, balance, carries and
technical obstacle skills. Access to purpose-built obstacles makes a real
difference when you are preparing for a race or working on technique.

Facilities in the directory include dedicated OCR gyms, outdoor obstacle
facilities, adventure centres, fitness facilities with OCR equipment, ninja and
obstacle centres, club-operated facilities, and seasonal venues.

Listings show the location, the type of facility, which obstacles are available,
indoor or outdoor, adult and youth sessions, open training availability, coaching
availability, and contact details.

## Training for your first race

You do not need access to every obstacle before entering your first event. General
running, strength and fitness work is a strong foundation on its own.

That said, practising the common obstacles — hanging and traversing, monkey bars
and rigs, rope climbing, walls, carries, balance and grip work — makes the day
considerably less of a surprise.

## Training competitively

Athletes preparing for competitive OCR benefit from facilities that allow specific
technical work for their discipline, whether that is 100m, 400m, Short Course or
Standard Course. See [rules](/compete/rules) for what each format demands.

## Nothing near you?

[Start a club](/clubs/start-a-club). It needs fewer people and far less equipment
than most people assume, and OCRA can connect you with existing clubs and coaches
while you get going.
