People come to obstacle racing with different backgrounds, experiences,
abilities and ambitions. Some want to compete internationally. Some want to finish
their first race, join a club, volunteer or coach. All of that is the sport.

## Our commitment

OCRA is committed to an environment where people are treated with dignity and
respect, and to reducing unnecessary barriers to taking part — in recreational
participation, competition, clubs, coaching, officiating, volunteering,
leadership, education and Team Ireland alike.

Participation should not be restricted because of someone's background,
ethnicity, nationality, gender, disability, sexual orientation, religion or
belief, socio-economic circumstances, previous sporting experience or current
level of ability.

## Adaptive and disability inclusion

Obstacle sport adapts well. Courses, obstacles and formats can be adjusted to
support athletes with different physical, sensory and other requirements.

OCRA wants adaptive athletes to have genuine opportunities to take part, train,
compete, develop, and represent Ireland where a competition pathway exists.
Coaches, clubs and organisers should consider accessibility when they plan, not
when someone asks.

## Inclusion and competitive categories are different things

An inclusive sport still has competition rules. Eligibility for a particular
category can depend on age, discipline, classification and the requirements of
international competition.

Those rules should be published clearly and applied consistently. What they should
never be is a surprise discovered at registration.

## What clubs and coaches can do

Consider accessibility and appropriate coaching adaptations. Use respectful
language. Plan for a range of abilities in the same session. Look honestly at what
stops people coming back, and at whether your club feels like somewhere a new
person can walk into.

## Discriminatory behaviour

Discriminatory, abusive or harassing behaviour is incompatible with taking part in
OCRA activities. Serious incidents are dealt with under
[Complaints & Disciplinary](/governance/complaints).

## Doing better than a statement

Saying everybody is welcome costs nothing and changes little. Making it true means
listening to participants, identifying the barriers that actually exist, and
changing how the sport is delivered.

OCRA wants to work with clubs, race organisers, coaches and community
organisations on that — particularly with people who do not currently see
themselves represented in the sport. If that is you,
[tell us what would help](/about/contact).
