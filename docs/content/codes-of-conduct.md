This is the OCRA Code of Conduct. It applies to everyone taking part in OCRA
activities: athletes, coaches, Technical Officials, volunteers, Team Ireland
members, club officers, event personnel, committee members, parents and
guardians, and spectators.

The first four sections apply to everyone. The sections after them set out what
comes additionally with a particular role.

## Respect

Treat everyone in the sport with dignity — other athletes, coaches, officials,
volunteers, organisers, parents, spectators and OCRA representatives alike.

## Fair play

Compete honestly. Cheating, deliberate rule violations, manipulating results or
seeking an unfair sporting advantage are incompatible with taking part in OCRA
competition.

## Abuse, harassment and discrimination

Abuse, harassment, bullying and discriminatory behaviour have no place in OCRA
activities. Depending on circumstances, concerns are handled under the
[complaints](/governance/complaints), [safeguarding](/governance/safeguarding) or
disciplinary procedures.

## Safety

Follow reasonable safety instructions and avoid behaviour that puts you or anyone
else at unnecessary risk. Coaches, officials, volunteers and event personnel carry
additional responsibility appropriate to their role.

## Athletes

Compete fairly and within the rules. Follow the instructions of Technical
Officials. Treat competitors, volunteers and organisers with respect. Avoid
abusive or threatening behaviour. Comply with
[anti-doping](/governance/anti-doping) requirements. Represent your club or
country in a way you would be content to have described back to you.

## Coaches

Coaches hold a position of trust, and the standard is correspondingly higher: put
athlete welfare first, maintain professional boundaries, coach safely, do not
abuse the authority the role gives you, respect officials, promote fair play,
follow safeguarding requirements, support clean sport, and keep personal interests
out of decisions about an athlete.

## Technical Officials

Act impartially and apply the rules consistently. Avoid favouritism. Declare
relevant conflicts of interest before the competition. Communicate
professionally, record decisions accurately, and treat athletes and fellow
officials with respect.

The integrity of a competition rests on athletes believing the same standard was
applied to everyone.

## Volunteers and event personnel

Follow the briefing and the instructions of event organisers. Treat athletes,
officials and other volunteers with respect. Work within your role and escalate
what falls outside it. Follow safeguarding requirements where the role involves
young people. Report incidents rather than resolving them informally.

## Team Ireland

Selected athletes carry the same standards as every other competitor, plus what
comes with representing the country: follow team management instructions, comply
with anti-doping obligations including the championship organiser's own, and use
national team clothing as required.

## Club officers and committee members

Act in the interests of the sport rather than personal advantage. Declare
conflicts of interest before taking part in a decision. Handle members'
information and club funds responsibly. Uphold safeguarding arrangements within
club activities.

## Parents, guardians and spectators

Support participants positively and respect athletes, coaches, officials and
volunteers. Aggressive, threatening or abusive behaviour towards athletes or
officials is not acceptable and can result in removal from an event.

## Breaches

Serious or repeated breaches are dealt with through the
[Complaints & Disciplinary](/governance/complaints) process.

Where a breach involves the welfare of a child, the
[safeguarding](/governance/safeguarding) route applies instead, and it takes
priority.
