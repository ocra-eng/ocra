Everything OCRA has for race organisers, in roughly the order you will need it. If
you are planning your first event, work down this page.

## Start here

[Event standards](/race-organisers/standards)
: Safety, risk assessment, obstacle safety, medical provision, emergency planning,
officials, rules, timing and athlete communication. Standards scale with the
event, so this is a practical baseline rather than a fixed bar.

[Event recognition & sanctioning](/race-organisers/sanctioning)
: What Listed, OCRA Sanctioned, Qualification Event and Irish National
Championship each mean, what the review involves, and how to apply.

[Work with OCRA](/race-organisers/work-with-ocra)
: The range of relationships available, from a calendar listing to a long-term
partnership.

[Submit an event](/race-organisers/submit-an-event)
: Get your event onto the national calendar.

## For competitive events

[Rules & regulations](/compete/rules)
: Current rulebooks by discipline. Athletes need to know which rules apply before
they race.

[Technical Officials](/education/technical-officials)
: What trained officials do, and how to discuss requirements for your event.

Results matter more than organisers expect, because they outlive the event — they
feed qualification, the [results archive](/compete/results) and the historical
record of the sport. If your event works formally with OCRA, agree result formats
and reporting before competition rather than improvising afterwards.

## Adding competition to a recreational event

You do not have to jump to National Championship standard. Competition can be
built up in stages, and most organisers are better off doing it that way:

1. Add competitive waves
2. Settle on consistent categories
3. Define your obstacle rules and publish them
4. Get timing you can rely on
5. Bring in trained Technical Officials
6. Publish results properly

Each step is useful on its own, and each one makes the next easier.

## Working with clubs

Local [clubs](/clubs/directory) are the shortest route to athletes who will
actually turn up. Clubs can help with promotion, volunteers, participation and
community engagement, and your event gives them something to train towards. It
works in both directions.

## Need help?

If you are planning an event and cannot find what you need,
[contact us](/about/contact).

We would much rather talk to an organiser early and work out what is required than
discover three days before race day that somebody has promised athletes a World
Championship qualification spot based on a sack race and optimism.
