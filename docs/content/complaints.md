OCRA maintains a disciplinary process so that alleged breaches of its rules,
ethics or standards of conduct are handled consistently rather than case by case.

## Before you write

Two things go somewhere else, and going there first is faster:

::include{from="_partials/safeguarding-escalation.md"}

A dispute about what happened *in* a competition — obstacle completion,
penalties, timing, results, an official's decision — is a **competition protest**,
not a complaint. Those follow the protest procedure for that event, which has a
time limit measured in minutes or hours. See [rules](/compete/rules).

## What can lead to disciplinary action

Cheating in competition. Abuse, harassment or discrimination. Dangerous behaviour
or safety rule violations. Serious breaches of OCRA rules. Conduct damaging to the
integrity of the sport. Misconduct by a coach or a Technical Official. Serious or
repeated breaches of the [Codes of Conduct](/governance/codes-of-conduct).

## Submitting a complaint

Complaints go in writing through the [contact form](/about/contact), under the
Complaint / Disciplinary Matter category.

Include your name, what happened, when and where, who was involved, any
witnesses, and any supporting documents, photographs or video. Anonymous
complaints are difficult to act on, because the person complained about has a
right to know the substance of the allegation.

## How it is handled

**First a review**, to establish whether the matter falls within OCRA's authority
and whether investigation is warranted. Some matters properly belong to a
competition protest, the safeguarding process, another sporting body, an event
organiser or a statutory authority — if so, you will be told where.

**Then investigation**, where it is warranted: reviewing the complaint, gathering
evidence, speaking to those involved and to witnesses, consulting event officials,
reviewing competition records and any video, against the applicable OCRA rules.

**A right to respond.** Anyone who is the subject of a complaint must be given the
opportunity to answer the allegations before a decision is made. A hearing may be
held remotely.

## Possible outcomes

No further action. A warning. Suspension. Removal from an OCRA role, a coaching
role, a Technical Official role or a committee position. Suspension or termination
of membership.

## Appeal

A person subject to a disciplinary decision may appeal to the OCRA Board within
**14 days**. Under the current Bylaws the Board's decision on appeal is final.
