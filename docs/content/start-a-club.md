A club does not need a large facility, a permanent obstacle course, or dozens of
members. Most successful sporting communities started as a small group of people
who wanted to train together.

If there is no OCR club near you, starting one is more achievable than it sounds.

## Why bother

A club creates regular local training, a social community, support for people
starting out, race preparation, youth and competitive development, coaching
opportunities, links to local events and facilities, and a way into the wider
Irish OCR community.

It also means the next person who moves to your area and searches for a club finds
one.

## You do not need your own obstacles

Plenty of useful OCR training needs no permanent course at all — running, strength
and conditioning, grip training, carries, climbing, bodyweight work, trail
running, and trips to existing [facilities](/clubs/directory).

Specialist obstacle training can be added as the club develops. It is not the
starting requirement.

## Getting going

The first steps are usually: bring together a small group of interested people,
find suitable training locations, establish regular sessions, agree how the club
will operate, write down basic rules and responsibilities, work out your safety
procedures, identify what coaching you need, deal with
[safeguarding](/governance/safeguarding) if children will be involved, and connect
with OCRA and other clubs.

## As it grows

A structure helps once there are more than a handful of you — someone
coordinating, someone on coaching, membership, finance, safeguarding, events and
communications. How formal that needs to be depends entirely on the size of the
club.

## Coaching

Provide training appropriate to the experience and ability of your members. As the
[coaching pathway](/coaching) develops, clubs will be encouraged to support their
coaches through recognised OCRA education.

## Youth participation

If you are running activities for children or young people, the
[safeguarding](/governance/safeguarding) arrangements need to be in place before
the activity starts. Policies, a Child Safeguarding Statement, risk assessment,
named roles, Garda Vetting where required, training, and a reporting procedure.

This is the part not to improvise.

## What OCRA can do

Connect you with other clubs, coaches, training facilities, events, Technical
Officials and national programmes — and once you are established, discuss
[affiliation](/clubs/affiliation).

## Thinking about it?

[Contact us](/about/contact). You do not need to have everything worked out first.

We would much rather help someone build a sensible structure from the beginning
than wait until they have written a constitution, bought seventeen tyres and
accidentally appointed their neighbour as Supreme Head Coach for Life.
