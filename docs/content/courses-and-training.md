## The two pathways

[Coaching](/coaching)
: Seven progressive levels, from Foundations through to Master Coach Trainer.
Covers coaches working with beginners, recreational athletes, competitive athletes
and international performers.

[Technical Officials](/education/technical-officials)
: Education and practical development for the people who apply competition rules
and keep racing fair.

## Technical Official training

TO education will combine the rules with the practical skill of applying them:
introductory education, competition rules, obstacle judging, penalties and
classifications, practical officiating, event operations, senior development, and
refresher sessions when rules change.

Practical experience at real competitions forms part of the pathway, because you
cannot learn to officiate in a classroom.

## Education that sits alongside the pathways

Some roles need knowledge that is not discipline-specific. Depending on your role
and level of responsibility, OCRA may provide, facilitate, or point you towards
safeguarding, first aid, disability awareness, inclusion, anti-doping, health and
safety, athlete welfare, club development and event operations training.

## How courses run

A mix of in-person workshops, online learning, practical sessions, mentored
experience, assessment and event-based development. Obstacle sport does not lend
itself to purely theoretical education, so most levels combine both.

## When courses appear here

Each listing will show the pathway and level, the date, whether it runs in person
or online, its duration, entry requirements, the fee where one applies, places
remaining, and the registration deadline.

## Recognition of Prior Learning

If you already hold relevant qualifications or substantial experience, whether
from obstacle sport or elsewhere, OCRA can consider it through Recognition of
Prior Learning. The detail is on the [coaching](/coaching) page.

Recognition does not remove the standards specific to the OCRA role you are
seeking.

## Continuing development

Refresher courses, rules updates, workshops, specialist modules, mentoring and
advanced education — so coaches and officials stay current as the sport changes.
