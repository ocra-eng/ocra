Obstacle Course Racing — OCR — combines running with a variety of physical and
technical obstacles. Depending on the event, athletes run, climb, carry, crawl,
balance, jump, swing and traverse their way through a course.

It tests running speed and endurance, grip and upper-body strength, balance,
agility, technique, problem solving and a fair amount of stubbornness. What makes
it different from most traditional sports is the variety: two courses can test
very different skills, and the fastest runner is not necessarily the fastest
obstacle racer.

## OCR is for everyone

Obstacle racing is not only an elite sport. Events range from accessible
recreational races built around taking part and enjoying it, to highly
competitive national and international championships.

People arrive from running, athletics, climbing, gymnastics, functional fitness
and strength sports — and plenty arrive having never competed in an organised
sport at all. There are opportunities for children, teenagers, adults, age-group
athletes, elite competitors and adaptive athletes.

You do not need to be able to complete every obstacle before you start. Obstacle
skills are learned, like any others.

## Competitive OCR

Competitive obstacle sport takes several forms.

### Short-format racing

Fast races testing speed, explosive movement and technical obstacle ability.
Formats such as 100 metre and 400 metre obstacle racing put athletes on compact
courses where small mistakes make a large difference.

### Short course

Running combined with multiple obstacles over a shorter endurance distance,
weighted towards the combination of running speed, obstacle efficiency and
endurance.

### Standard course

Longer races requiring athletes to sustain endurance while still negotiating
obstacles successfully throughout the course.

### Team and relay

Athletes can also compete in team and relay formats, with the structure
depending on the competition.

## Recreational and endurance OCR

Many obstacle races are built around participation, personal challenge and
community rather than championship competition. They range from short
introductory races to endurance events covering serious distance and terrain.

## Obstacles

Obstacle design varies between events, but the common challenges are hanging and
traversing obstacles like monkey bars and rigs, walls and rope climbs, balance
obstacles, crawls, carries, technical grip obstacles, and the over-under-through
family.

Competitive events set specific rules for how an obstacle must be attempted and
what counts as completing it — see [Rules & Regulations](/compete/rules).

## Getting started

You do not need to begin with competition. For most people the best introduction
is finding a local community and trying it.

- Find a [club or gym](/clubs/directory) near you
- Enter a recreational [event](/compete/events)
- [Join OCRA](/membership)
- Train towards national or international competition, through
  [qualification](/compete/qualification)
- Become a [coach](/coaching), [Technical Official](/education/technical-officials)
  or [volunteer](/get-involved/volunteer)
